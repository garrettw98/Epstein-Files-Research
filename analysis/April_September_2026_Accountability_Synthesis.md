# April-September 2026 Accountability Synthesis

> **Window**: March 17 - September 23, 2026
> **Purpose**: explain how six months of developments after the March escalation changed the shape of the Epstein-files story
> **Method**: every factual statement below is tied to a dated item in `updates/live_events.latest.txt`, the [Government Response Timeline](../timeline/Government_Response_To_Epstein_Files.md), or the linked topic files. Allegations are labeled as allegations; denials are noted.

## Summary

The March phase was about *control of the residual archive*. The April-September phase is about *enforcement*: who can be compelled to produce the rest of the record, and who is now exposed because of what was already produced.

Five tracks define the period:

1. **DOJ leadership changed hands, but the files did not stop being contested.** Bondi was removed (Apr 2), Blanche was confirmed (Aug 8), and the files were central to the confirmation fight.
2. **Courts became the primary enforcement venue for the Transparency Act.** *Phang v. Blanche* produced the first judicial orders requiring DOJ to unredact or justify, and a New York judge ruled the Act overrides old sealing orders.
3. **House Oversight completed a witness program and hit its first open defiance.** Eleven interviews ran from April to July; Leon Black was held in contempt on Sept 16.
4. **Criminal exposure reappeared.** The WSJ reported (Sept 23) that SDNY is investigating the estate's co-executors. Congress sent DOJ abuse-allegation referrals. State and foreign prosecutors kept cases open.
5. **The transnational fallout reached heads of government and royal houses.** Andrew and Mandelson remain under investigation, Starmer resigned, Norway appointed a commission while Mette-Marit became queen, and France's probe widened after a key suspect died.

**Inference (labeled):** Removing Bondi did not change DOJ's litigation posture. The same arguments about redactions continued under Blanche, who is now the named defendant in *Phang v. Blanche*. The personnel change moved the question of responsibility rather than resolving it.

## 2. Courts Took Over Enforcement

Before April, the only enforcement of the Transparency Act was political: floor speeches, letters, and subpoenas. After April, it became judicial.

- **Phang v. Blanche (D.D.C.)**: preliminary injunction (Jun 25), in camera review of 10 records (Jul 25, when DOJ admitted redacting one of Epstein's own email addresses), contempt warning from the bench (Aug 13), and a Sept 24 deadline after the court called DOJ's position "without merit" (Sep 16), with a companion order for the "Jane Doe 4" FBI interview notes. DOJ filed a protective notice of appeal in August, appealed the Sep 16 order (Sep 17), and asked the D.C. Circuit to stay the foreign-language-records portions (Sep 22). Separately, survivors sued DOJ and Google over exposed personal information (Mar 26), and a magistrate judge recommended that survivors' $320 million FBI negligence suit proceed (Aug 18).
- **Maxwell case (S.D.N.Y.)**: Judge Preska held (Aug 11) that the Act overrides prior sealing orders, grand-jury secrecy, and Maxwell's privacy claims, finding that continued secrecy would leave the public with "an incomplete and potentially misleading picture."
- **Watchdogs**: the DOJ Inspector General (Apr 23) and GAO (Apr 28) opened reviews of the redaction process. Neither has reported.

**Why it matters:** The categories under court review (hidden email correspondents including the "torture video" thread, the unfiled 2007 draft indictment's co-conspirator names, FBI interview notes including an unverified allegation involving Trump, and non-English records) are the same categories this repo has tracked as the highest-value gaps since February. See [Phang v. Blanche Litigation](../topics/The_Phang_v_Blanche_Litigation.md), [The Draft Indictment](../topics/The_Draft_Indictment.md), and [The Redaction and Context Scandal](../topics/The_Redaction_and_Context_Scandal.md).

## 3. The House Witness Program: What It Produced and What It Didn't

The committee's March 3 request list (Gates, Black, Waitt, Ruemmler, Groff, Kellen, Band) was completed, supplemented by Lutnick, Noel, Bondi, and Staley. See [House Oversight Witness Program (2026)](../topics/The_House_Oversight_Witness_Program_2026.md).

**New information on the record:**
- **Sarah Kellen** (May 21) described more than a decade of abuse by Epstein and made first-time allegations against Maxwell and two men in Epstein's orbit. Comer asked DOJ (Jun 4) to investigate Philip Levine and Frederic Fekkai, including through witness immunity. **Both deny the allegations.**
- **Bill Gates** (Jun 10) said Epstein used knowledge of his affairs to pressure him and that emails suggest Epstein "contemplated blackmailing me." The Gates Foundation's external review (Jul 21) found roughly 30 meetings from 2011 to 2014, no payments to Epstein, and no knowledge of his trafficking.
- **Jes Staley** (Jul 24; transcript Aug 26) said he shared confidential and market-sensitive JPMorgan information with Epstein.
- **Ted Waitt** (Apr 30) said he paid Maxwell $7.2 million after their breakup.
- **Kathryn Ruemmler** (Jul 15) said Epstein referrals made her former law firm "single-digit millions."

**Limits the minority has flagged:** witnesses were generally interviewed, not deposed under oath, and video was released only for the Clintons (NOTUS, Aug 6). Groff's account has been disputed by survivors (CNN, Jul 9). Doug Band repeatedly said he could not recall. Leon Black ended his interview after refusing NDA questions, then defied subpoenas and sued.

**Refusals:** besides Leon Black, former Epstein lawyer **Alan Dershowitz**, who had publicly volunteered to testify, withdrew five days before his Jul 20 interview citing an unspecified legal barrier; Comer demanded a Sep 2 appearance, and no outcome has been reported.

**Still outstanding:** the public survivor hearings Comer promised (Apr 10, Jul 1) and the committee's final report with the criminal referrals he said he "fully expect[s]."

The civil class settlement over the same executors' alleged role (up to $35 million) had a final-approval hearing on Sep 16; no ruling was located as of Sep 23.

## 5. The U.S. Is Now the Bottleneck for Foreign Cases

The Washington Post (Sep 9) reported that investigators in Poland (waiting since April), Norway, and Latvia have received no response to mutual legal assistance requests, and that British investigators are still seeking records for the Mandelson case. Separate reporting (Aug 22) said the Met's Mandelson inquiry could stall without unredacted files. New Mexico, a U.S. state, is suing DOJ for the same reason. DOJ says it "has not refused to assist any jurisdiction."

**Inference (labeled):** The same unredacted material at issue in *Phang v. Blanche* is what foreign and state prosecutors say they need. That makes court-ordered disclosure more than a transparency issue: it may determine whether several criminal cases can proceed at all.

## 6. Money-Channel Evidence Hardened

- **Wyden's "Looking the Other Way" report (Aug 4)** concluded a four-year investigation. It said more than a dozen bankers at JPMorgan, Bank of America, and Deutsche Bank knew of suspicious Epstein transactions as early as 2002, that Deutsche failed to promptly report more than $250 million including payments to women in Russia and Eastern Europe, and that about $400 million moved through BNY Mellon in 270 wires.
- **Bank of America** settled for $72.5 million (final approval Aug 27), the third bank to settle publicly after JPMorgan and Deutsche Bank. BofA denies facilitating Epstein's crimes.
- **Staley's testimony** and Sen. Warren's July letter to Jamie Dimon reopened questions about JPMorgan's senior-level knowledge. JPMorgan maintains Dimon did not know Epstein.

See [The Banks](../topics/The_Banks.md) and [The Money Trail](../topics/The_Money_Trail.md).

## 7. Politics: The Files Became an Electoral Variable

- **Massie**, the Act's lead Republican sponsor, lost his May 19 primary to a Trump-backed challenger in the most expensive House primary on record, then used his remaining term to launch EFTA II. Its discharge petition reached 218 signatures on Sep 18.
- Speaker Johnson canceled late-September session weeks and recessed early (Sep 16), which Massie said was designed to delay the vote. A vote is possible only after the House returns on Nov 9.
- **Ohio's Senate race** turned partly on Les Wexner's donations to Sen. Jon Husted (AP, Sep 23).
- **Stacey Plaskett**, the Virgin Islands delegate who survived a November 2025 censure vote over texting Epstein during a 2019 hearing, won the Democratic primary for Virgin Islands governor with about 48% (Aug 1-3). See [Stacey Plaskett](../profiles/Stacey_Plaskett.md).
- A GBAO poll reported that 88% of likely midterm voters, including 83% of Republicans, would prioritize candidates promising accountability, including full release of the files.
- **Melania Trump** (Apr 9) publicly denied any Epstein ties and called for survivor hearings. Survivors' reactions were mixed.

## 8. Institutions Beyond Government: Academia

Universities ran their own reviews. Bard's president **Leon Botstein** retired (announced May 1) after a WilmerHale review; Columbia disciplined dental-school officials over an "irregular" admission tied to Epstein's girlfriend and Nobel laureate Richard Axel gave up an institute directorship; Ohio State's review cleared a physician of involvement in crimes; Harvard (Nowak) and Yale (Gelernter) reviews remain open. House Judiciary Democrats extended their university inquiry to Harvard and Bard on Jun 17. See [The Academic Fallout (2026)](../topics/The_Academic_Fallout_2026.md).

## 9. Survivors Took the Fight to Civil Court

New York City's reopened claims window (veto overridden Jan 29, open until about July 2027) turned into a second enforcement track run by survivors rather than governments. It produced suits against Les Wexner (dropped without prejudice Sep 1 over jurisdiction), FirstBank Puerto Rico (Jun 24), the estate and executors (Aug 19), hairstylist Frédéric Fekkai (Sep 1), and the estate over Epstein's abuse-image collection (Sep 15-16). Earlier cases targeted DOJ and Google (Mar 26) and the FBI (Aug 18 recommendation to proceed). Defendants deny the claims. See [The Survivor Litigation Wave (2026)](../topics/The_Survivor_Litigation_Wave_2026.md).

**Inference (labeled):** Civil suits reach people and institutions that criminal cases have not, and they produce discovery. They also settle, so they rarely produce public findings about who knew what.

## 10. Naming Without Charging

On Aug 31 Rep. Massie read 14 names on the House floor, extending February's reading of six DOJ-redacted names. Floor speeches are protected from defamation claims, so they put names on the record without testing the evidence. Those named who have responded deny wrongdoing; two of the 14 are dead. See [The Massie Floor Speech](../topics/The_Massie_Floor_Speech_Aug_2026.md). Italy opened its own inquiry (Sep 5), with two named Italians publicly discussed.

## Working Narrative

The strongest current synthesis:

> By September 2026 the Epstein-files story has become an enforcement contest with four fronts: a federal judge compelling DOJ disclosure, a House committee testing its subpoena power against a billionaire, state and foreign prosecutors who say U.S. records are the missing link, and a newly reported federal probe of the two men who have controlled Epstein's money and records since 2019.

That is a significant shift from February, when DOJ could credibly say the files were out and the matter was closed. The archive is still incomplete, but for the first time several institutions with coercive power are working on the gaps simultaneously.
