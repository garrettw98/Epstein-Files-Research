# Pam Bondi (Former U.S. Attorney General)

> **Status**: **FORMER GOVERNMENT OFFICIAL** — U.S. Attorney General Feb 2025 - Apr 2, 2026 (removed by President Trump)
> **Role in Repo**: Release governance, congressional oversight testimony, House Oversight witness
> **Last updated**: Sep 23, 2026

## Summary

Pam Bondi was U.S. Attorney General during the 2025-2026 transparency phase. In this repository she is central to release sequencing, redaction disputes, and congressional oversight. After the House Oversight Committee subpoenaed her in March 2026, President Trump removed her on April 2. She then declined the deposition in her former official capacity, and on May 29 she sat for a closed-door transcribed interview instead.

## Key Timeline

- **Feb 27, 2025**: DOJ OPA published "first phase" declassified-file release language under Bondi's office.
- **Jan 30, 2026**: DOJ announced publication of 3.5 million responsive pages under the Transparency Act.
- **Feb 11, 2026**: Testified before House Judiciary for more than five hours; questioned by members of both parties.
- **Feb 12, 2026**: AP reported a dispute over DOJ logging of congressional file-portal searches; Bondi denied ordering monitoring.
- **Mar 4, 2026**: House Oversight voted to subpoena her, with five Republicans joining Democrats.
- **Mar 17, 2026**: Oversight issued the subpoena for an **April 14** deposition. [Subpoena cover letter](https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/03.17.2026-Subpoena-Cover-Letter-Bondi-FINAL.pdf), [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/03/17/epstein-files-bondi-subpoena/)
- **Apr 2, 2026**: **Removed as attorney general.** Trump said she would move to the private sector; reporting cited frustration that included her handling of the Epstein files. Todd Blanche became acting AG. [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/02/pam-bondi-attorney-general-out), [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-pam-bondi-attorney-general/)
- **Apr 8, 2026**: DOJ said she would not appear because she had been subpoenaed in her official capacity. Ranking Member Robert Garcia said the subpoena applied to her "whether she is the Attorney General or not," and Democrats pushed contempt. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/08/politics/pam-bondi-epstein-deposition-justice-department), [Axios](https://axios.com/2026/04/08/pam-bondi-epstein-house-oversight-subpoena)
- **Apr 29, 2026**: Agreed to testify on May 29. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/04/29/politics/bondi-deposition-epstein-house-oversight)
- **May 29, 2026**: Sat for a roughly **four-hour transcribed interview** (not a deposition, not video recorded) under an arrangement with Chairman Comer. The released transcript shows her declining many questions, denying an integral role in managing the release, and pointing to Blanche. [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/29/nx-s1-5836563/pam-bondi-epstein-congress), [The Hill transcript](https://thehill.com/homenews/house/5910872-oversight-committee-bondi-transcript/)
- **Jul 15, 2026**: Vice President Vance said her claim about having Epstein binders on her desk made "people mistrust the entire effort." [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/15/jd-vance-epstein-files-joe-rogan-podcast)

## How Her Role Changed

- **February**: public face of release-era process.
- **March**: subpoena target; the dispute became about the accountability of identified officials.
- **April-May**: removed from office, then a former official testifying about decisions made on her watch, with the key question of who decided what now split between her and Blanche.

## What Is Documented vs Unresolved

### Documented
- She was the listed witness at the Feb 11 House Judiciary hearing.
- DOJ release actions through April 2 occurred under her authority.
- She was removed on April 2 and gave a transcribed interview on May 29.

### Unresolved/Disputed
- Why she was removed. Blanche said only Trump knows; reporting cited several factors, including the Epstein files.
- Whether release and redaction choices were operational constraints, legal constraints, or policy decisions, and who made them.
- Whether the DOJ Inspector General and GAO reviews will attribute specific decisions to her.
