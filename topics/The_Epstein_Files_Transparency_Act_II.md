# The Epstein Files Transparency Act II (H.R. 9694)

> **Status**: **DISCHARGE PETITION COMPLETE, AWAITING FLOOR VOTE** (Evidence Level: ● PRIMARY LEGISLATIVE RECORD)
> **House sponsors**: Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY), Rep. Ro Khanna (D-CA), Rep. Teresa Leger Fernández (D-NM)
> **Senate sponsors**: Sen. Jeff Merkley (D-OR), Sen. Ben Ray Luján (D-NM)
> **Introduced**: July 15, 2026
> **Last updated**: Sep 23, 2026

## Purpose

The original [Epstein Files Transparency Act](The_Epstein_Files_Transparency_Act.md) (H.R. 4405, Pub. L. 119-38) ordered DOJ to publish its Epstein records but gave no one outside the executive branch a clear way to enforce it. The sponsors say DOJ has continued to withhold more than 3 million pages and to over-redact what it released. EFTA II is designed to add enforcement.

## Key Provisions (as described by sponsors and reporting)

- **Private right of action**: state attorneys general, district attorneys, Epstein victims, and members of Congress could sue the U.S. attorney general for failing to comply with the original Act.
- **State prosecutor access**: gives state attorneys general access to DOJ's unredacted files to pursue alleged co-conspirators under state law.
- **Victim access**: requires the attorney general to provide unredacted documents to victims on request.
