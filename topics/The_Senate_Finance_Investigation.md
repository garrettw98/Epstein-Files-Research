# The Senate Finance Investigation (Wyden)

> **Status**: **ONGOING (MINORITY INVESTIGATION)** — four years old as of 2026; findings referred to House Oversight (Jun 4, 2026); DEA "Chain Reaction" memo and Treasury bank records still not public
> **Last updated**: Sep 24, 2026

## Summary

Sen. Ron Wyden (D-Ore.) began investigating Epstein's finances in 2022 as Senate Finance Committee chair and continued as ranking member after January 2025. The investigation follows the money: Leon Black's payments, banks' anti-money-laundering reporting, the Treasury Department's suspicious-activity records, and a Drug Enforcement Administration financial probe that DOJ has not fully released. As a minority investigation it has no subpoena power, so much of its 2026 work was letters, floor requests, referrals, and a final report. **Wyden's descriptions of documents are his characterizations.** Responses from the people and agencies involved are recorded below.

## Open Items (as of Sep 24, 2026)

- **The Chain Reaction memo**: The unredacted memo has not been made public. DOJ says members of Congress can read it in its reading room. What the probe found, why it ended without charges, and who the 14 others were remain unknown. This repo does not guess at the redacted names.
- **Treasury records**: No production to Congress has been reported, and the records bill remains blocked.
- **Regulatory follow-up**: No regulator has publicly announced action on the Aug 4 report's recommendations.

## Why It Matters

This is the only congressional track focused mainly on money rather than names or testimony. Its central claim is that financial institutions and federal agencies saw red flags for years without acting. Its central limit is that, as a minority investigation, it depends on what agencies and institutions choose to hand over.
