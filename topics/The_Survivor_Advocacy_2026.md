# Survivor Advocacy (2026)

> **Status**: **ONGOING** — survivors are working through Congress, the courts, foreign governments, public exhibits, and the midterm campaign
> **Last updated**: Sep 24, 2026

## Summary

In 2026 Epstein survivors and their families became organized participants in the fight over the files rather than only witnesses. They criticized DOJ's release for exposing their identities while shielding others, testified to Congress, sued, pressed foreign leaders, and took the released records on tour. This page collects those actions. Survivors' statements are their accounts. Where they name or accuse others, the accused's denials are recorded on the relevant pages.

## Patterns

- **Identity exposure is the survivors' central grievance against DOJ.** Their demand is not less disclosure, but disclosure that protects them while naming facilitators.
- **Survivors increasingly choose the venue.** Congress (both parties), civil courts, foreign leaders, and public exhibits all became pressure points once federal prosecution stalled.
- **The issue is now partisan campaign material.** That increases attention but also the risk that survivors' accounts are used for purposes they did not choose.
